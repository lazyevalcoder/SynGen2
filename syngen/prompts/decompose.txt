You are a senior Revenue Operations analyst. You will be given a business narrative. Your job is to reverse-engineer it into measurable acceptance criteria that a generated dataset must satisfy for the story to be true.

Rules:
- Every criterion MUST be computable from tabular data (accounts and opportunities tables). No subjective criteria.
- Give each criterion a target value and a tolerance.
- Flag any claim in the story that is ambiguous or under-specified, and state the assumption you chose to resolve it.
- Classify each criterion as either "parametric" (fixable by adjusting distribution parameters) or "statistical" (needs sample size or seed changes).

User decisions already made (override any default you would otherwise choose):
{{user_decisions}}

Story:
{{story}}

Context you may assume:
- Data model: accounts table + opportunities table (list price, realized price, discount pct, close date, stage, region, segment, owner). When the story references products/SKUs/tiers/margins/COGS, opportunities also carry product_id, product_tier (e.g., entry/core/premium) and cogs_ratio. When the story references territories or sales regions grouped into areas, both tables carry territory. When the story references plans/quotas, a quota_plan sheet (per-segment OR per-territory quarterly revenue targets) is generated too and revenue_vs_plan measures attainment against it.
- Product tiers carry configurable gross margins and optional per-quarter COGS inflation; discount levels can be coupled to tier margin (WS4 correlation).

Respond with ONLY a JSON object:
{
  "criteria": [
    {
      "id": "AC1",
      "name": "short name matching the definition exactly",

      "params": { ... check-specific params with target and tolerance ... },

    {"issue": "...", "resolution": "..."}

The optional "depends_on" array lists IDs of criteria whose targets are
mathematically linked to this criterion's target, such that changing one
requires re-reviewing the other. Example: realized_vs_list (realized/list
percentage) is the complement of avg_discount_quarter for the same quarter,
so it must depend on that criterion. Only add edges you are confident about;
empty array when none.

Available checks and their params:
- win_rate_flat: {band_pp}
- avg_discount_quarter: {quarter, target_pct, tolerance_pp}
- discount_trend_monotonic: {max_dip_pp}
- region_discount_premium: {region, vs: [regions], min_premium_pp, quarters: [labels]}
- end_of_quarter_effect: {window_days, min_gap_pp}
- realized_vs_list: {quarter_start, target_start_pct, quarter_end, target_end_pct, tolerance_pp}
- data_sanity: {max_discount_pct}
- cycle_length_trend: {min_increase_pct} - average won-deal cycle time (close minus created, days) must grow from first to last quarter by at least this percent. Use for "deals are taking longer to close".
- creation_volume_trend: {target_decline_pct, tolerance_pp} - opportunity creation change from first to last quarter lands within tolerance of the claimed decline (story says pipeline slowed ~20% -> target_decline_pct=20, tolerance_pp=8).
- deal_size_trend: {target_change_pct, tolerance_pp} - average won-deal size (list price) change from first to last quarter lands within tolerance of the claim. Story says deal sizes shrank ~15% -> target_change_pct=-15.
- icp_creation_shift: {min_increase_pp} - share of created pipeline from NON-ICP accounts must rise by at least this many percentage points from first to last quarter. Use for "pipeline growth came disproportionately from low-quality accounts".
- revenue_concentration: {top_n, min_top_share_pct} - top N deals' share of last-quarter won revenue must be at least this percent. Use for "revenue depended on a handful of large deals".
- revenue_vs_plan: {segment, target_pct, band_pct, dimension?} - attainment of quarterly revenue plan vs quota targets. Use when the story references quotas/plans or "missed/exceeded plan by X%". target_pct is the story's expected attainment: missed plan by ~5% -> target_pct=95; beat by ~4% -> target_pct=104. band_pct is tolerance (2 is a good default). segment may be a named segment/territory OR "_all_" for company-wide attainment claims (e.g., "revenue finished at 101% of plan"). For territory plans add "dimension": "territory". Do not invent other aggregate names like "Total" or "Overall" - use "_all_" for those claims.
- blended_margin_trend: {target_change_pct, tolerance_pp} - blended gross margin % of won deals changes from first to last quarter within tolerance of the claim. COGS rising -> margin falling: "gross margin eroded ~3 points" -> target_change_pct=-3, tolerance_pp=1.
- tier_share_shift: {tier, from_share_pct, to_share_pct, tolerance_pp} - one product tier's share of WON REVENUE moves from ~from_share_pct (first quarter) to ~to_share_pct (last quarter). Use for mix-shift claims ("entry-tier products grew to 40% of sales", "mix moved to premium").
- discount_margin_link: {high_margin_tier, low_margin_tier, min_gap_pp} - avg discount on the high-margin tier must exceed the low-margin tier's by at least min_gap_pp. Use for "discounts were concentrated in high-margin products".
- avg_price_by_tier: {tier, max_avg_realized_usd} - average realized price of won deals in a tier stays under the cap. Use for entry-price / price-point claims ("SMB bought only entry-level deals around $15k").
- gap_concentration: {dimension, min_bottom_gap_share_pct} - the bottom quartile of plan units (by attainment) must hold at least this share of total plan shortfall. Use for "the miss was concentrated in weak territories" (dimension: territory).

IMPORTANT semantics:
- realized_vs_list measures REVENUE RETAINED as a percentage of list price. Its targets are approximately 100 minus the discount percentages (e.g., discounts rising 12% to 18% means realized/list falls ~88% to ~82%), NOT the discount values themselves.
- Only reference regions/segments that plausibly exist in the data model (e.g., AMER, EMEA, APAC). Do not invent extra regions or tier names - when the story implies tiers without naming them, use entry/core/premium and say so in ambiguities.
- Margin claims are percentage-point changes (3 points of margin = target_change_pct=-3), NOT percent changes.
