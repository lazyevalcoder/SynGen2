DRAFTING CONSTRAINTS (authoring guide - follow before writing any criterion)

These rules come from hard limits of the data engine. A criterion that
breaks them cannot be built and will be sent back or escalated.

1. SCOPE ONLY REAL UNITS.
   Every region / segment / tier / territory / unit you name must exist in
   the data model. Never invent a unit, and never use a pseudo-cohort as a
   unit (e.g. a "segment" like core_ex_whales, "small_territories", or
   "top accounts"). Express subset claims with the checks' cohort/split
   params (e.g. exclude_outlier_deals, cohort top_pct/bottom_pct), or scope
   to a real unit. Use '_all_' where the check allows it.
   To express a "core vs headline" split (the core business missed plan
   while whales masked it), use revenue_vs_plan with
   exclude_outlier_deals=true and segment '_all_' (and, if needed,
   dimension for the plan sub-block). Do NOT pass dimension='ex_outliers' -
   'ex_outliers' is not a plan dimension; the engine splits headline vs
   core via exclude_outlier_deals.

2. MATCH THE CLAIM'S DIRECTION.
   A "miss" is not a "beat"; a "decline" is not "growth"; a margin move is
   in percentage POINTS, not percent. Respect the SIGNED conventions of
   each param (see the generated facts below).

3. NEVER DRAFT A THRESHOLD THAT CANNOT FAIL.
   A cap no data can approach, a min_gap_pp of 0, or a one-sided bound that
   excludes nothing is vacuous - it "passes" without proving the story and
   will be rejected. Give every criterion a bound the data could plausibly
   violate.

4. STAY WITHIN WHAT THE ENGINE CAN BUILD.
   Revenue is raked to plan, so quantities that are plan-of-record (or
   pinned by raking) cannot be moved by distribution knobs. Do not demand a
   magnitude no lever can reach; prefer the reachable form of the SAME
   claim. An "attainment beat vs plan" is NOT a "growth" claim; an
   unreachable concentration level is still provable at a reachable level
   or with a larger top_n. When unsure, choose the conservative reachable
   target and record the assumption in "ambiguities".

5. PREFER ONE CRITERION PER CLAIM.
   Do not draft several criteria that pin the same coordinate to different
   non-overlapping bands - the set is jointly unsatisfiable and will be
   rejected. If two claims share a coordinate, they must share a band or be
   scoped to different units.

6. RANGE-BOUND POINT-ESTIMATE CLAIMS (quality, not just correctness).
   When the story states a magnitude ("healthy at 3.5x", "concentrated in a
   few deals", "declined ~6 points"), encode that magnitude as a RANGE
   (target +/- band, or a min AND a max) - not a bare lower bound. A
   one-sided ">= X" can pass by overshooting wildly (e.g. coverage "healthy
   at 3.5x" passing at 65x): it lands but does not match the story, and a
   real reviewer would reject the dataset. Checks that support an upper
   bound: coverage_ratio (max_multiple), pipeline_concentration and
   revenue_concentration (max_top_share_pct), slippage_trend
   (max_increase_pp). Set the bound the story's magnitude implies.
